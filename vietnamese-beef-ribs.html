Vietnamese Style Ketchup Beef Ribs

越式茄汁牛肋條
Serves6–8
Prep~20 minutes
Braise1 hour, low
DifficultyEasy

The name says ketchup, but there is no ketchup in this pot. The tomato comes from a can of paste and two cans of whole San Marzano tomatoes — a slower, sweeter, less sugary base. Beef fingers go into a dry hot pan first to take on colour, then everything braises low with lemongrass, ginger, star anise and a block of rock candy until the meat gives way. A Vietnamese home-kitchen braise: the spice bag does the heavy lifting, and the fish sauce goes in only at the end.

01

Ingredients

The Pot

Beef fingers (frozen)4–5 lb
Carrots, cut into ~¾″ rounds6
Ginger slices, skin on15
Tomato paste1 can
San Marzano whole tomatoes2 cans
Lemongrass (tops and bottoms trimmed, halved into short stalks)3 stalks
Purple onion, sliced1
Yellow onion, sliced1
Water3 cups
Oil, for the pot4 tbsp
No oil for the sear: the beef fingers go into a dry, medium-hot pan. They carry enough fat of their own — adding oil only makes them steam instead of colouring.
Two sizes: this recipe is written for 4–5 lb of beef fingers. Working with a 2 lb pack? Use the ½ option in the scaling box at the bottom — it drops every ingredient for you and serves 3–4.

The Spice Bag

Star anise5
Cinnamon stick1
Dried chili peppers4
Bay leaves5
Bag it: whole spices go into a spice bag so they can be lifted out cleanly after an hour. Loose star anise left in the pot keeps getting stronger.

Seasoning — At The End

Fish sauce4 tbsp
Black pepperto taste
Rock candy (sugar)1 large block
Last, not first: the fish sauce and pepper are added only once the beef is tender, so their salt doesn't concentrate during the long braise.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    Meat Prep
    Defrost the beef fingers, then rinse them. Dry each strip with a paper towel — dry meat is what browns — and cut into 2″ long strips.
  2. 2
    Carrots
    Cut the carrots into medium pieces, around ¾″ thick. Thick rounds hold their shape through an hour of braising; thin coins turn to mush.
  3. 3
    Sear the Beef
    Sear the beef fingers lightly on both sides in a hot pan. No oil. Medium heat — you want colour on the outside, not cooked-through meat.
  4. 4
    Build the Braise
    In a large pot, heat 4 tbsp oil and lightly stir-fry the lemongrass and the sliced onions over medium heat. Add the tomato paste and cook it for 1 minute — that minute is what cooks the raw tinny edge out of it. Add the two cans of San Marzano tomatoes and 3 cups of water. Put the spices into a spice bag and place it in the pot. Put all the carrots on top, add the rock candy, cover, and cook over medium-to-low heat for 1 hour.
    ⚠️ Medium-to-low means a slow, lazy bubble — not a rolling boil. A hard boil will tighten the meat and cloud the sauce.
  5. 5
    Test, Season, Serve
    After one hour, test the beef fingers — they should give way without resistance. Add the fish sauce and black pepper, then simmer for 5 minutes. Remove the spice bag and the lemongrass, and serve.
03

Chef's Notes

🍖

The Tenderness Test

Check the beef at the one-hour mark before you season anything. If a strip still resists, give it another 15–20 minutes — timing depends on how thick the strips are and how hard the simmer is.

🐟

Fish Sauce Goes In Last

Four tablespoons, added only after the meat is tender. Salt that goes in at the start concentrates through an hour of reduction; salt added at the end lands exactly where you want it.

🌿

Lemongrass & the Bag

Trim the tops and bottoms off the stalks and halve them into short pieces. Both the lemongrass and the spice bag come out before serving — after an hour they've given everything they have.

🍬

The Rock Candy

One large block is the only sweetness in the pot, and its job is to round off the acidity of the tomatoes. If the sauce still tastes sharp at the end, that's the lever to reach for.

Scaling This Recipe

Scale the ingredients proportionally. The one-hour braise is a starting point, not a rule — always decide with the tenderness test.
