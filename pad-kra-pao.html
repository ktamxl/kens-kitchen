Recipe No. 008 · Thai · Street Food Classic

Pad Kra Pao

泰式打拋豬
Serves2
Prep5 minutes
Cook10 minutes
DifficultyEasy

Thailand's national comfort food, and the fastest dinner in this collection. Minced pork, garlic, shallots and Thai chilis go into a screaming-hot wok, a one-bowl sauce of oyster, fish, soy and dark soy follows, and holy basil is tossed through at the very last second until it barely wilts. Fifteen minutes, start to finish. Serve it over jasmine rice with a crispy fried egg on top — that runny yolk is not decoration, it cuts the heat.

01

Ingredients

The Stir-Fry

Thai chili peppers, thinly sliced1–3
Garlic, minced6 cloves
Shallots, minced2
Vegetable oil (or any neutral oil)3 tbsp
Ground pork (80% lean / 20% fat)1 lb
Holy basil (or Italian basil)1¼ cups
Chili math: the seeds are the fire. De-seed and use fewer for a mild version; smash the chilis and garlic together in a mortar and pestle if you want it properly hot — that releases more of the aromatic oils.

The Stir-Fry Sauce

Oyster sauce1 tbsp
Fish sauce1 tbsp
Soy sauce (Golden Mountain preferred)2 tsp
Dark soy sauce1 tsp
Sugar1 tbsp
Chicken broth, low-sodium⅓ cup
Mix it before you turn on the wok. Everything after this happens in minutes — there is no time to measure sauce once the pork hits the pan.

To Serve

Fried egg1 per serving
Steamed jasmine riceto serve
Holy basil is hard to find. Reach for Italian basil before Thai basil — its sweet, slightly peppery profile is much closer to holy basil than Thai basil's heavy anise notes.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    Mix the Sauce
    In a bowl, combine the oyster sauce, soy sauce, fish sauce, dark soy sauce and sugar. Stir until the sugar has dissolved and set it beside the wok.
  2. 2
    Sauté the Aromatics
    Heat the vegetable oil in a wok or large skillet over medium-high until hot. Add the shallots, garlic and Thai chilis and sauté until fragrant — about 45 seconds. Don't brown them; you want perfume, not bitterness.
  3. 3
    Sear the Pork
    Turn the heat up to high, add the ground pork, break it into smaller pieces and toss to combine with the aromatics. Then spread it into a single layer and leave it alone to sear. Toss again and keep cooking until the pork is cooked through, lightly charred and all the liquid has evaporated — about 4–5 minutes.
    ⚠️ The sear is the whole flavour of this dish. Crowd the pan or stir it constantly and the pork steams grey instead of caramelising.
  4. 4
    Sauce and Deglaze
    Pour in the sauce and toss to coat. Then deglaze the pan with the chicken broth, scraping the base, and cook until it reduces down and disappears into the pork — about 1 minute.
  5. 5
    Wilt the Basil
    Add the basil and toss until it is just wilted — seconds, not minutes. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
  6. 6
    Serve
    Spoon over steamed jasmine rice and top with a crispy fried egg with a runny yolk. Break the yolk through the pork as you eat.
03

Chef's Notes

🌶️

Dial the Heat Both Ways

Less spice: fewer chilis, and scrape out the seeds — that's where the burn lives. More: pound the chilis and garlic together in a mortar and pestle first, which bruises them and releases far more oil.

🍳

Don't Skip the Egg

Fried hard in plenty of oil so the edges go lacy and crisp, yolk still liquid. Dropped on top, it melts through the pork and takes the edge off the chili.

🌿

Which Basil?

Holy basil is peppery with a hint of clove — the real thing, if you can find it. Thai basil is anise-heavy and not a great match. Italian basil is the closest everyday substitute.

🫙

Leftovers

Keeps up to 4 days in an airtight container in the fridge. Reheat in a skillet over medium heat, or covered in the microwave. It's just as good the next day over rice.

Scaling This Recipe

Easy to double — but the wok is not. Sear the pork in batches so every batch gets a hot pan.
