Creamy Garlic Mashed Potatoes

蒜香薯蓉
Serves8 as a side
Prep15 minutes
Cook15 minutes
DifficultyEasy

The Thanksgiving side, done properly. Yukon Gold potatoes simmered until a fork slides through, then mashed while they're still hot with garlic-infused milk — the trick that carries the flavour through every spoonful instead of leaving it sitting on top. Softened butter and a modest spoonful of sour cream finish it, and a pat of butter plus a fistful of chives go on at the table. Thirty minutes, and no lumps if you follow the one rule: mash hot, stop mixing.

01

Ingredients

The Potatoes

Yukon Gold potatoes (about 10), peeled and quartered4 lb
Fine sea salt1½ tsp (or to taste)
Pick the right potato. Yukon Gold are waxy — naturally buttery and creamy rather than floury. Red potatoes work well too. Plan on about ½ lb of potatoes per person when building the menu.

The Garlic Milk

Whole milk (or half and half)1⅓ cups
Garlic cloves, smashed6
Why milk, not cream alone? The garlic steeps in warm milk and the flavour goes through the whole pot. Any milk works, but whole milk is what makes them rich.

To Finish & Serve

Unsalted butter, softened8 tbsp
Sour cream¼ cup
Chives, finely chopped, to garnish2 tbsp
Extra butter, for the topa pat or two
Soften the butter ahead. Room-softened butter stirred in at the end gives more butter flavour than melted butter poured in — and the sour cream, even at just ¼ cup, is what people taste and can't place.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    Peel and Cut
    Rinse the potatoes and peel them, removing any dark spots. Keep the peeled potatoes in cold water as you go — that stops them browning and rinses off excess starch. Cut into halves or quarters, depending on size.
  2. 2
    Boil Until Fork-Tender
    Drain and transfer to a pot — stainless steel if you have it, so you can cook and mash in the same vessel. Cover with cold water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook 15–18 minutes, until a fork goes in easily. Larger pieces take longer.
  3. 3
    Steep the Garlic Milk
    While the potatoes finish, smash 6 garlic cloves with the side of a knife and put them in a small saucepan with the 1⅓ cups of milk. Warm over medium-low, stirring occasionally, until you just see steam — then take it off the heat.
    ⚠️ Do not let the milk boil. Once it boils it can catch and turn, and the flavour goes flat.
  4. 4
    Drain and Mash Hot
    Drain the potatoes thoroughly. If they still look moist, set the pot over very low heat while you work. Mash right away, while they're hot — cold potatoes turn gummy. A potato masher, a ricer (zero lumps), or an electric hand mixer all work; mash coarsely for now.
  5. 5
    Add the Garlic Milk
    Pour the hot milk in through a small strainer so the garlic cloves stay out, adding it until you reach the consistency you like. Keep mashing until smooth and creamy — then stop. Overworking potatoes makes them gluey. Scrape the sides and bottom of the pot with a spatula to catch any hidden chunks.
  6. 6
    Butter, Sour Cream, Salt
    Add the 8 tbsp softened butter and the ¼ cup sour cream, then 1½ tsp salt (or to taste). Whip until creamy and taste for salt.
  7. 7
    Serve
    Spoon into a serving bowl and lay a pat or two of softened butter over the top while they're hot — it melts in and keeps the surface from drying. Finish with a generous shower of chopped chives.
    ⚠️ Not serving straight away? Leave them in the pot over very low heat and mash occasionally — they stay hot and steamy instead of stiffening up.
03

Chef's Notes

🧈

Softened, Not Melted

Both work, but butter at room temperature stirred through hot potatoes gives noticeably more flavour than melted butter poured in. Take it out of the fridge before you start peeling.

🥣

The Sour Cream

Only a quarter cup, and yet it's the thing people notice without being able to name. It adds tang and richness that butter alone can't. Skip it if you must — but don't skip it by choice.

⏱️

Get Ahead

Peel and cut the potatoes the day before and keep them submerged in cold water in the fridge — then cook when you're ready. To hold them for serving, put the mashed potatoes in a buttered slow cooker on low for up to 4 hours, stirring occasionally and loosening with warm milk if they dry out.

🍳

Reheat & Leftovers

Reheat in a buttered skillet over medium heat, stirring, until steamy. And if you have leftovers, they make outstanding cheesy potato pancakes — the good kind of second life.

Scaling This Recipe

Roughly ½ lb of potatoes per person. Everything else scales straight up — just warm the milk gently and in batches if you double it.
