Roasted Cornish Game Hens with Lemon & Herbs

檸檬香草燒春雞
Serves2–4
Marinade2 hrs – overnight
Roast425°F · ~30 min
DifficultyEasy

Two small birds, flattened, rubbed with garlic, lemon, thyme, oregano and paprika, then roasted hot and finished hotter. Spatchcocking is the whole trick: a bird opened out flat cooks evenly in half the time and gives up far more crisp skin than one left trussed in a ball. The vegetables go in underneath to catch everything that drips, and the last ten minutes at 475°F is what turns the skin lacquered and crackling. Simple enough for a weeknight, good enough to serve to people.

01

Ingredients

The Birds & The Pan

Cornish game hens2
Onion, sliced1
Carrots, sliced2
Cherry tomatoes (optional)a handful
Lemon, halved½
Cornish game hens are the easy-to-find equivalent. The original recipe uses poussins (spring chickens) — Cornish game hens are what you'll find in a US supermarket, and they're a little larger. If yours are over about 2 lb, add 5–10 minutes to the first roast.
Versus the written recipe: the creator's written list says ½ a carrot, but the video uses two for two birds. Use as much as will cover the base of your tray — the vegetables are there to flavour the pan and soak up the drippings.

The Marinade

Olive oil2 tbsp
Garlic cloves, minced4
Fresh thyme4 sprigs
Paprika3 tsp
Dried oregano2 tsp
Saltto taste
Black pepperto taste
Brush it on thoroughly. Get the marinade into the joints, under the wings and along the cut where the backbone came out — that's where the flavour actually reaches the meat.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    Spatchcock the Birds
    With kitchen shears, cut down both sides of the spine and lift the backbone out — work along the spine, never through the breast. Turn the bird over and press down flat on the breastbone until it lies open like a book.
    ⚠️ All the cutting happens along the spine. Cut into the breast and you lose the juices and the neat shape.
  2. 2
    Wash and Dry
    Rinse the birds and then dry them thoroughly with kitchen towels, inside and out. Dry skin is the entire difference between crisp and flabby.
  3. 3
    Make the Marinade
    Mince the 4 garlic cloves (a garlic press is fine), strip the thyme leaves, and mix with the paprika, oregano, olive oil, salt, pepper and the juice and zest of the lemon half.
  4. 4
    Marinate
    Brush the marinade over both birds, working it into the joints and the cavity. Cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours — overnight is better.
  5. 5
    Prep the Vegetables
    Before the birds go in the oven, slice the onion and carrots and sauté them for about 3 minutes until fragrant — just enough to take the raw edge off. Cherry tomatoes are optional; drop them in whole.
  6. 6
    Into the Tray
    Lay the vegetables across the roasting pan, sit the birds on top, skin side up, and give them another sprinkle of paprika, salt and cracked pepper.
  7. 7
    Roast — 425°F for 30 Minutes
    Into a preheated oven at 425°F for about 30 minutes. Cornish game hens are small, so this is most of the cooking done.
  8. 8
    Blast the Skin — 475°F for 5–10 Minutes
    Turn the oven up to 475°F for another 5–10 minutes, until the skin is golden and crisp. Watch it — at that temperature the line between crisp and burnt is short.
  9. 9
    Rest, Then Serve
    Let the birds rest for about 10 minutes out of the oven, then plate them with the roasted vegetables and a little lemon zest grated over the top.
03

Chef's Notes

🔪

Why Spatchcock

A flattened bird cooks evenly instead of leaving the breast dry while the thigh catches up. It also means more skin facing the heat — which is the point of the whole exercise.

🌡️

Two Temperatures, One Skin

425°F does the cooking; 475°F does the finish. If you only have one setting, stay at 425 and accept slightly paler skin — but don't try to do the whole roast at 475 or the garlic and paprika will scorch.

🍋

Lemon at Both Ends

Half the lemon goes into the marinade, the zest goes over the finished plate. That second hit of citrus is what keeps the dish from tasting heavy after all that paprika.

🥕

The Pan Vegetables

Don't throw them out — they've been basted in chicken fat for half an hour. Serve them under the bird, or blitz them with the pan juices into a rough sauce.

Scaling This Recipe

Two birds feed two generously or four with sides. The marinade scales straight up; the oven settings do not change.
