Herb Crusted Rack of Lamb

香草脆皮羊架
Serves2
Prep~15 minutes
Oven375°F · 5–8 min
DifficultyIntermediate

The restaurant plate that teaches you to sear properly. A rack of lamb gets frenched, seasoned hard, seared on its fat cap with butter, garlic and thyme — then painted all over with Dijon and packed with a basil, parsley, parmesan and panko crust before a short blast in a hot oven. The mustard is the quiet hero: it glues the crust on and cuts the richness of the meat. Medium rare is the whole point here, and that means a thermometer, not a clock.

01

Ingredients

The Lamb

Rack of lamb (8 bones)~2 lb
Thyme, fresh1 bunch
Garlic1 head
Butter2–3 tbsp
Olive oil, for the pana little
Coarse salt & cracked black peppergenerous
Frenching: the butcher usually removes most of the fat cap — frenched just means trimming the remaining fat and membrane back so the bones are exposed and clean. Do it with a paring knife: tedious, but simple.

The Herb Crust

Basil leaves, packed¾ cup
Flat-leaf parsley, fresh½ cup
Parmesan, grated¾ cup
Salt1½ tbsp
Panko bread crumbs1 cup
Dijon mustard, for brushinggenerous
Make extra: this crust is not a one-trick thing — it works beautifully on chicken and fish too. Double the batch and keep the spare in the freezer.

The Sauce

Olive oil1 tbsp
Shallot, minced1
Aged balsamic vinegar¼ cup
Chicken broth½ cup
Butter1 tbsp
Salt & pepperto taste
Optional: the sauce is a nice-to-have, not a requirement. A quick red-wine reduction built on the pan drippings does the same job.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    French the Rack
    Trim the fat cap back and expose the bones — that's all frenching means. A paring knife is the only tool you need. Take your time; a clean rack sears and carves better.
  2. 2
    Season Hard
    Coarse salt and freshly cracked pepper, rubbed into every surface — including the parts you won't eat. That's what builds the crust when it hits the pan.
  3. 3
    Sear on the Fat Cap
    A little olive oil in the pan, brought up to temperature. Start by rendering the fat cap — hold the rack up for a minute or so until it colours, then the back side, then the ends. Tongs help, and remember the bones heat up fast. After a few minutes add butter, the thyme and the whole head of garlic, flip the rack back over and use the herbs and garlic as a trivet.
    ⚠️ The fat cap spits once it starts rendering — keep your hands back and keep the heat moderate. You want deep colour, not a burnt crust.
  4. 4
    Paint It With Dijon
    Colour on all sides? Out of the pan. Brush Dijon mustard over the whole rack — top, sides and bottom. Don't be stingy. Give it a moment to absorb: the mustard both glues the crust on and brings a sharp counterpoint to the lamb.
  5. 5
    Build & Pack the Crust
    In a food processor, combine the ¾ cup basil, ½ cup parsley, ¾ cup parmesan, 1½ tbsp salt and 1 cup panko. Blend until evenly combined, then tip it out onto a plate. Dab the rack into it — all sides, top and bottom — and sprinkle a little extra over the back.
  6. 6
    Short, Hot Roast
    Back into the pan and into an oven preheated to 375°F. Cooking time varies with the size of the rack, but generally it's only 5–8 minutes. Use a meat thermometer: for medium rare, pull it at 125°F internal.
    ⚠️ Small window, big consequences. A 2 lb rack goes from perfect to grey in a couple of minutes — set a timer for 4 minutes and start checking.
  7. 7
    Rest, Carve, Serve
    Let it rest before slicing — then cut straight down the middle between the bones. Served here with puréed red-garlic potatoes and carrots, and the balsamic sauce from the ingredients list.
03

Chef's Notes

🌡️

Trust the Thermometer

A 5–8 minute roast leaves no room for guesswork. 125°F internal is medium rare; it will climb another few degrees while it rests. Worth knowing: the narration says 135°F, but the uploader's own written correction says 125°F — 125°F is the number to cook to.

🌿

The Crust Isn't Just for Lamb

Basil, parsley, parmesan and panko is a genuinely useful house mix. Press it onto chicken breasts or a fillet of fish the same way and it lifts an everyday dinner into something else.

🥣

Be Generous With the Mustard

A thin smear won't hold the crust. Give it a proper coat on the top, the sides and the bottom — that layer is doing two jobs at once: adhesive and flavour.

🍷

Sauce, Two Ways

Written version: soften a minced shallot in olive oil, add ¼ cup aged balsamic and ½ cup chicken broth, reduce, finish with butter. The video's alternative is a quick red-wine reduction off the pan drippings. Both optional, both good.

Scaling This Recipe

One rack feeds two. Scale it up and sear the racks one at a time — crowding the pan steams the meat instead of colouring it.
