Scrambled Egg Shrimp Fried Ho Fun

滑蛋蝦仁炒河
Serves2
Prep~15 minutes
Cook~10 minutes
DifficultyIntermediate

Order it once in a cha chaan teng and you'll order it again — flat rice noodles charred hard in the wok, then buried under a glossy shrimp sauce with silky ribbons of scrambled egg stirred through it. The ingredients are cheap and there are only two real skills: getting wok hei into the noodles without breaking them, and thickening the sauce so it clings. Everything else is timing. The sauce has to 掛得起 — hang on the noodles. If it slides off, it isn't thick enough yet.

01

Ingredients

The Noodles & Vegetables

Fresh ho fun (flat rice noodles)~400 g
Silver sprouts (bean sprouts — Ipoh sprouts are ideal)a small handful
Yellow chives (韭黃)a handful
Scallion, choppedto taste
Light soy sauce~½ tbsp
Fresh noodles only. Dried ho fun will not survive this dish — you want the soft, oily sheets from the fridge section. They usually come stuck together; tear them apart by hand before you start.

The Shrimp & The Egg Sauce

Shrimp, peeled (31–40 count per lb)~10
Egg1
Chicken broth (store-bought is fine)~1 cup
Potato starch slurry (生粉水)as needed
Salt~⅓ tsp
Sugara pinch
Flaky salt, for the shrimpa pinch
Oil, for the woka circle of it
Potato starch, not cornflour. The chef is specific: use potato starch slurry for the egg sauce. It gives the clear, glossy cling this dish needs.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    Loosen the Ho Fun
    The noodles come pressed together. Tear them apart slowly by hand — gently, strand by strand.
    ⚠️ Don't grab and pull at them. Ho fun breaks if you rush it, and broken noodles won't char properly.
  2. 2
    The Anti-Stick Trick
    Heat the wok until it is very hot, then turn the heat down low and add a circle of oil. Pour a little beaten egg — about a quarter of the egg — over the noodles. This is the trick: egg aroma, and nothing sticks.
  3. 3
    Fry the Noodle Base
    Turn the heat to high and add the noodles. For the first minute, do not toss the wok — let them sit and take on a little char. Then add the sprouts, followed by the yellow chives — don't overcook them — and about ½ tbsp light soy. From here, fold from the bottom up so the noodles don't break. Fry until dry and smelling of wok hei, then plate.
    ⚠️ Vigorous tossing from the start is the classic mistake: the noodles shatter and you never build enough heat for wok hei.
  4. 4
    Pan-Fry the Shrimp
    No marinade. Straight into the pan — pan-frying gives a far richer shrimp aroma than blanching. Add a pinch of flaky salt, fry one side until half-cooked, then turn and fry the other.
  5. 5
    Build the Sauce
    Pour in the chicken broth and add about ⅓ tsp salt. When it's nearly boiling, return the shrimp to the pan and add a pinch of sugar.
  6. 6
    Thicken — Then Test
    Turn the heat down, add the potato starch slurry a little at a time while stirring, then turn the heat back up. Only at a boil does the starch gelatinise — and only then can you see whether the sauce is thick enough.
  7. 7
    Drop the Egg
    Beat the egg lightly — leave a little egg white in it. Hold the bowl high and pour it in a thin stream; if you pour from low the ribbons come out coarse. Let it set for 8–10 seconds, then push gently with a ladle so it breaks into soft pieces. Scallion goes in last, off the heat.
    ⚠️ Beaten smooth, the egg turns the whole sauce flat yellow and loses its silkiness. Leave some white.
  8. 8
    Plate It
    Pour the sauce over the noodles. It should cling — 掛得起. If it runs straight off, the sauce isn't thick enough: stir in a little more slurry and bring it back to a boil.
03

Chef's Notes

🔥

Wok Hei Is the Dish

Treat the noodles exactly as you would dry-fried beef ho fun — just without the beef and with far less dark soy. Dry, charred and smoky is the target; wet and pale is a failure.

🥚

The Quarter-Egg Trick

A little beaten egg on the noodles before they go in is what stops them sticking to the wok. It works on fried rice, vermicelli and silver needle noodles too — worth remembering.

🍤

Never Blanch the Shrimp

Restaurants blanch because it's fast, and the flavour goes down the sink with the water. Pan-frying these takes two extra minutes and tastes like a completely different ingredient.

🥢

Bigger Shrimp Welcome

Don't feel locked into small ones — prawns or shrimp balls work just as well, and need only a little more pan-frying time before the sauce goes in.

Scaling This Recipe

Everything scales, but the wok doesn't — fry the noodles in batches so each one gets a properly hot, uncrowded pan.
