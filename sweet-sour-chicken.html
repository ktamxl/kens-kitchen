Sweet and Sour Chicken

甜酸雞
Serves4
Prep25 minutes
Cook15 minutes
DifficultyMedium

The takeout classic, made the way a restaurant makes it. Chicken breast in a wet batter, fried twice — 350°F to cook it, 380°F to make it crunch — then folded into a sauce built in the wok from ketchup, red rice vinegar, rice vinegar and sugar and tightened with potato starch. Daddy Lau cooked this dish several times a day for sixty years; the technique is not delicate, but the order of operations is exact. The one move home cooks miss: the heat goes off before the chicken goes in.

01

Ingredients

The Chicken & Marinade

Chicken breast 雞胸 — about 12 oz340 g
Oyster sauce 蠔油1 tbsp
Light soy sauce 生抽1 tsp
Cornstarch 生粉2 tbsp
Water1 tbsp
Salt1 tsp
Breast, cut against the grain. Twelve ounces makes four generous portions. The cornstarch in the marinade is not there to thicken — it holds water in the meat so it stays juicy through two visits to the oil.

The Batter

Plain flour4 tbsp
Cornstarch 生粉2½ tbsp
Baking powder1 tsp
Water7 tbsp
Neutral oil1 tsp
The baking powder is doing real work. It aerates the batter in the hot oil, which is what gives the coating its bubbled, crunchy shell rather than a smooth jacket.

The Sweet & Sour Sauce

Ketchup 茄汁5 tbsp
Red rice vinegar 大紅浙醋3 tbsp
Rice vinegar 米醋1 tbsp
Sugar 糖4 tbsp
Salt½ tsp
Potato starch 生粉, for the slurry2 tsp
Water, for the slurry2 tbsp
Mix the slurry twice. Potato starch settles to a brick within a minute. Give it a stir immediately before it goes into the wok, or you will pour water in and leave the starch behind.

The Vegetables

Pineapple 菠蘿, fresh or canned170 g
Carrot 甘筍55 g
Red bell pepper 紅椒85 g
Onion 洋蔥55 g
Corn or peanut oil, for frying — about 10 oz300 ml
Fresh pineapple if you can, canned if you can't. Drain canned pineapple well — the syrup is sweet enough to tip the sauce. The oil is for frying and is not eaten; 10 oz gives enough depth in a wok for the chicken to float free.
02

Method

  1. 1
    Cut the Vegetables into Triangles
    Slice the pineapple into ½-inch slabs. Peel the carrot and cut it into short oblique segments, then stand each one up and slice it ¼-inch thick. Cut the pepper and onion into triangles roughly the size of the chicken. Flat faces and sharp corners are the point — they catch the sauce; a dice just rolls around in it.
  2. 2
    Marinate the Chicken
    Slice the breast ½-inch thick, cut each slice into strips, then into 1-inch chunks. Push the meat to one side of the bowl, put the oyster sauce, light soy, cornstarch, water and salt in the other side, mix the marinade with itself first, then fold it through the chicken. Leave it while you make the batter and the sauce — 15 minutes is enough.
  3. 3
    Mix the Batter
    Whisk the flour, cornstarch and baking powder together, then add the water a little at a time, stirring until there are no lumps. You want a batter that coats the back of a spoon and whose stream narrows as it falls. Stir in the 1 tsp oil last.
    ⚠️ Too thin and it slides off the chicken in the oil, leaving bare meat. If it pours like milk, sift in more flour and cornstarch — a tablespoon at a time.
  4. 4
    Stir the Sauce and the Slurry
    Two small bowls. In the first, whisk the ketchup, red vinegar, rice vinegar, sugar and salt until the sugar dissolves. In the second, mix the potato starch and water — stir it again just before it goes into the wok, because it settles.
  5. 5
    First Fry — 350°F
    Heat the oil to 350°F in a wok. Pour the batter over the chicken and turn it with chopsticks until every piece is coated, then lower the pieces in one at a time, close to the surface. Fry in batches — 2–3 minutes — until pale gold, and lift them on to a rack. Skim out any loose batter between batches so it does not burn and bitter the oil.
    ⚠️ Crowd the wok and the oil temperature drops; the batter drinks oil and the chicken goes greasy instead of crisp.
  6. 6
    Second Fry — 380°F, Then the Vegetables
    Bring the oil up to 380°F and put the chicken back in for 30 seconds — that is the crunch. Lift it out, then drop the carrot, pepper and onion into the same oil for 30 seconds and take them out. Pour the oil off and wipe the wok.
  7. 7
    Sauce in the Wok, Chicken Off the Heat
    Put the wok back on high with the sauce. When it boils, stir it, drop to medium and add half the slurry slowly, stirring, until it turns glossy and clings to the spatula. Add the vegetables and turn them through. Turn the heat off. Add the chicken and fold to coat, then the pineapple, then a drizzle of oil. Serve immediately.
    ⚠️ Chicken goes into the sauce with the heat off. On the heat, the sauce steams the crust and the whole point of the double fry disappears.
03

Chef's Notes

🍗

A Wet Batter, Like the Restaurants

The dry-cornstarch coating used for sweet and sour pork makes a thin shell. This batter — flour, cornstarch, baking powder and water — puffs in the oil and holds sauce without going soft. It is why takeout chicken looks the way it does.

🔥

Fry It Twice

The first fry at 350°F cooks the meat through and sets the coating. Thirty seconds at 380°F drives the last of the moisture out of the crust. Skip the second fry and the chicken is done, but it is not crisp — and it will not stay crisp under sauce.

🧴

Ketchup Is the Base, Not a Shortcut

Ketchup brings body, sweetness and a little acidity in one ingredient — that is why it is in the sauce rather than tomato paste, which is too thick and too sharp. Five tablespoons to four of sugar is the balance.

🍶

Two Vinegars

Red rice vinegar gives the colour and the rounder tang; plain rice vinegar sharpens it. Using only rice vinegar gives you a pale, thin sauce that tastes like vinegar rather than sweet and sour.

⏱️

Heat Off Before the Chicken

The sauce is finished before the chicken arrives. Folded in off the heat, the coating stays crisp under the glaze; left on the flame for even a minute, it softens and the dish collapses.

🍍

Pineapple Last

It goes in at the very end, off the heat, so it stays cold and sharp against the hot sauce. Cooked pineapple turns to jam and sweetens everything around it.

Scaling This Recipe

Doubling the chicken is easy; doubling the frying is not. Fry in more batches rather than a fuller wok, and keep the oil at temperature between them.
