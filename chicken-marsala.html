Chicken Marsala

Pollo al Marsala
Serves4
Total Time~30 minutes
DifficultyEasy
Serve WithButter-Parmesan Linguine

Marsala is the trick Italian-American kitchens have been pulling on home cooks for a century: one bottle of fortified wine turns a plain pan-seared cutlet into something that tastes like a Sunday dinner. This is the weeknight version — four cutlets, one wide pan, mushrooms browned properly, and a sauce thickened just enough to coat a spoon. Serve it over butter-parmesan linguine and it stops being a chicken dish and becomes a plate worth repeating.

01

Ingredients

Chicken & Sauce

Chicken breasts (butterflied into 4 cutlets)2 large
All-purpose flour, for dredging½ cup
Salt & pepper (mixed into the dredging flour)to taste
Olive oilfor the pan
Cremini mushrooms1 lb
Garlic, chopped8 cloves
Shallots, finely diced (red or yellow onion works too)2
Butter, for the mushrooms2 tbsp
All-purpose flour, for the sauce~2 tbsp
Marsala wine1⅓ cups
Chicken stock~1 cup
Heavy cream~⅔ cup
The pan is the recipe: don't crowd it. Four cutlets in one pan steam instead of browning — two batches in a wide skillet beats one crowded pan every time.

Butter-Parmesan Noodles

Linguine1 lb
Butter4 tbsp
Parmesan, grated (plus more to serve)~1 cup
Reserved starchy pasta water1.5–2 cups
Salt & pepperto taste
Timing: put the water on when the chicken hits the pan — the pasta should finish at the same moment as the sauce.
02

Instructions

  1. 1
    Butterfly the Chicken
    Cut each breast horizontally through the middle, open it up, and split into 2 pieces. Put each piece in a Ziploc bag and gently flatten to even thickness — a wine bottle works as a mallet.
  2. 2
    Dredge
    Coat both sides of each cutlet in the seasoned flour.
  3. 3
    Sear
    Heat a good glug of olive oil in a wide pan until shimmering (test it: sprinkle in a little flour — it should bubble and brown). Cook the cutlets in two batches so the pan is never crowded: about 3 minutes on the first side, 2 minutes on the second, until golden. Remove and rest; add fresh oil between batches.
  4. 4
    Prep the Mushrooms
    Brush off any dirt with a towel and snap out the stems, then slice. Same pan back on the heat; cook until soft and nicely coloured, a few minutes. Add the butter.
    ⚠️ Don't wash the mushrooms. Water makes them steam and go rubbery — wipe them clean instead.
  5. 5
    Aromatics
    Push the mushrooms aside, add a touch more oil, then the garlic and shallots. Cook 35–45 seconds, just until fragrant — no longer.
  6. 6
    Thicken
    Sprinkle about 2 tbsp flour over everything and stir it in.
  7. 7
    Deglaze
    Add the Marsala, scraping up all the browned bits from the bottom of the pan as the sauce starts to thicken.
  8. 8
    Stock
    Add the chicken stock and bring it to a simmer.
  9. 9
    Cream
    Add the cream and simmer until the sauce thickens — it's ready when a spatula dragged across the pan leaves a trail that doesn't immediately fill in.
  10. 10
    Reunite
    Nestle all 4 cutlets back in, spoon sauce over the top, and keep everything on low while you finish the noodles.
  11. 11
    Noodles
    Drain the pasta and return it to the pot with the butter; mix to melt. Add the parmesan and splashes of the reserved pasta water until glossy and creamy. Season with salt and pepper.
  12. 12
    Plate
    Noodles down first, chicken on top with plenty of extra sauce. Finish with cracked pepper; parsley or more parmesan if you like.
03

Chef's Notes

🍄

Cooking for a Crowd

Two pans help: a wide skillet or a Dutch oven can sear all four cutlets in one go. Otherwise work in batches — don't crowd the pan or the chicken steams instead of browning.

🍷

Marsala Math

1⅓ cups is just over half a standard bottle, so one ~$15 bottle covers the whole recipe. Port, brandy, or white wine plus a splash of brandy all substitute fine.

🍝

Linguine, From Dry

Big pot — at least 4 quarts of water for a full pound. Salt well once boiling (1.5–2 tbsp kosher salt). Boil 1 minute less than the package says; the noodles finish softening under the hot sauce. Reserve the starchy water before draining, and never rinse.

🥄

The Sauce Test

Stock and cream are estimates — start where the recipe says and adjust until the sauce coats a spoon the way you like it. At double volume it simply needs an extra minute or two.

Scaling This Recipe

Cooking for more? Scale the ingredients proportionally. Sear in batches and give the sauce a little extra time — it thickens more slowly at volume.
